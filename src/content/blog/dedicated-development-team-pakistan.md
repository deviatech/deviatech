A dedicated development team is not simply a list of developers at a monthly rate. It is a working arrangement around a backlog, technical ownership, communication rhythm, code quality, and the capacity you actually need. Pakistan can offer strong technical capacity, but the engagement still needs structure.

## When a dedicated team makes sense

It can work when you already have a product owner, a prioritised backlog, a repository, and a reason to add capacity for several delivery cycles. It is less suitable when the product problem and scope are still unknown; that is usually a discovery or fixed-scope project first.

## What to evaluate

### Technical fit

Check experience with the actual stack and workflow, not a generic list of technologies. DeviaTech works with React, Next.js, Go, Electron, Laravel, Docker, and related web product tooling. The [Cvilo case study](/case-studies/cvilo-ai-resume-builder) shows a Next.js and React product alongside Go and AI capabilities.

### Communication

Ask who attends planning, where questions are recorded, how often progress is shared, and which time-zone overlap is guaranteed. Weekly updates are useful only when they show working progress and decisions.

### Ownership

Your team should retain access to the repository, deployment, analytics, and project context. Define pull requests, review responsibility, documentation, and what happens if the engagement ends.

### Capacity and cost

Pricing depends on seniority, weekly capacity, engagement length, technical ownership, and whether the team is expected to join product decisions. Do not compare only hourly rates; compare the amount of usable delivery capacity and review work you must provide.

## A sensible onboarding process

Start with codebase and backlog review, agree on the team shape and working hours, then use a small first delivery cycle to test the collaboration. Only increase capacity after the team understands your product and your review process.
