Most software shops in Lahore quote by the hour. That can be appropriate for open-ended product work, but it means **you**, the client, carry more of the risk when the scope is still uncertain. If the estimate is wrong, you pay for the extra time.

At DeviaTech, a project starts with a short discovery call, then a written scope and a fixed price before a single line of code is written. If something changes mid-project, we re-scope it explicitly rather than quietly running the meter.

This post explains why we work that way, how the two pricing models compare, and what to check in any quote before you sign. If you are earlier in the process and still comparing vendors, see [how to choose a web development company in Pakistan](/blog/web-development-company-pakistan).

## Fixed price vs hourly: who carries the risk

| | Fixed price | Hourly / time and materials |
|---|---|---|
| Who carries estimation risk | The agency | The client |
| What you know before starting | Total price and deliverables | An hourly rate and an estimate |
| Best suited to | A scope that can be written down | Open-ended or continuously changing product work |
| How change is handled | Explicit re-scope with its own price | Absorbed into the running total |
| Main failure mode | Scope written too vaguely to hold | Budget drifts without anyone deciding to spend more |
| What you should still demand | Written assumptions and exclusions | Weekly visibility into hours and output |

Neither model is dishonest. The problem is a quote that gives you the *uncertainty* of hourly with none of the flexibility, or a fixed price with no written assumptions behind it.

## Six questions to ask before choosing an agency

### 1. What exactly is included?

Ask for the pages, flows, integrations, testing, deployment, content responsibilities, and handoff deliverables. "A complete website" is not a usable scope.

### 2. Who will actually do the work?

You should know whether you are speaking to the people building the project, an account manager, or a rotating outsourced team. Direct communication is especially valuable when requirements are changing.

### 3. How will progress be reviewed?

Weekly working increments are more useful than a large reveal at the end. Ask where you will see the work and how decisions are recorded.

### 4. Who owns the code and accounts?

Your repository, domain, hosting, analytics, Shopify store, and other accounts should not be held hostage by the agency. Put ownership and handoff in writing.

### 5. What happens after launch?

Ask about the bug-fix window, support availability, deployment access, monitoring, and the price of future changes.

### 6. What does the price not include?

Domain, hosting, paid SaaS tools, payment-provider fees, copywriting, product photography, ads, and third-party usage can sit outside the build quote. The agency should say so clearly.

## What a sensible Pakistan project range looks like

DeviaTech's focused Shopify projects start from **PKR 40,000**, while focused custom software and MVP projects start from **PKR 150,000**. These are not comparable packages: the final range depends on the number of screens and flows, integrations, roles, content, testing, and post-launch support.

For a Pakistani business, also ask how the agency will handle COD operations, local payment constraints, courier or shipping workflows, mobile performance, and communication across your working hours. A technically impressive build that your team cannot operate is not a successful project.

## Why a written scope protects both sides

Most of our clients aren't technical, and they're used to negotiating a price up front for physical goods and services. A software quote should work the same way — you should know exactly what you're paying before you commit.

## What it means for you

- No surprise invoices.
- A written scope you can hold us to.
- If the project turns out to need more than expected, that's our estimation mistake to absorb — not yours to pay for.

## Red flags worth taking seriously

- A very low quote with no written assumptions.
- A promise to build a large platform in an unrealistically short timeline.
- No clear answer about code, account, or deployment ownership.
- A portfolio made only of screenshots with no explanation of the problem or scope.
- No named person responsible for communication after payment.
