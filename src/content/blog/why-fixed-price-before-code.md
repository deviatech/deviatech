Most software shops in Lahore quote by the hour. That can be the right structure for open-ended product work, but be clear about what it means: when the scope is uncertain, **you** carry the risk. If the estimate turns out to be wrong, you pay for the extra time.

We do it the other way round. A project starts with a short discovery call, then a written scope and a fixed price, agreed before anyone writes a line of code. When something changes mid-project, we re-scope it out loud instead of quietly running the meter.

This post covers why, how the two models compare, and what to check in either kind of quote. If you're earlier in the process and still shortlisting vendors, [how to choose a web development company in Pakistan](/blog/web-development-company-pakistan) is the better starting point.

## Who carries the risk

| | Fixed price | Hourly / time and materials |
|---|---|---|
| Who carries estimation risk | The agency | The client |
| What you know before starting | Total price and deliverables | An hourly rate and an estimate |
| Best suited to | A scope that can be written down | Open-ended or continuously changing product work |
| How change is handled | Explicit re-scope with its own price | Absorbed into the running total |
| Main failure mode | Scope written too vaguely to hold | Budget drifts without anyone deciding to spend more |
| What you should still demand | Written assumptions and exclusions | Weekly visibility into hours and output |

Neither model is dishonest. The bad outcome is a quote that gives you the uncertainty of hourly with none of its flexibility, or a fixed price with no written assumptions holding it up.

## Six questions to ask before choosing an agency

### 1. What exactly is included?

Ask for the pages, flows, integrations, testing, deployment, content responsibilities and handoff deliverables. "A complete website" is not a scope, it's a mood.

### 2. Who will actually do the work?

You should know whether you're talking to the people who will build it, an account manager, or a rotating cast of subcontractors. This matters most when requirements change, which they will.

### 3. How will progress be reviewed?

Weekly working increments beat a grand reveal at the end. Ask where you'll see the work and how decisions get recorded, because verbal agreements evaporate around week five.

### 4. Who owns the code and accounts?

Your repository, domain, hosting, analytics and Shopify store should never be leverage in a disagreement. Put ownership and handoff in writing before you pay anything.

### 5. What happens after launch?

Ask about the bug-fix window, support availability, deployment access, monitoring, and what future changes cost. Launch day is not the end of the relationship, and the price of month two should not be a surprise.

### 6. What does the price not include?

Domain, hosting, paid SaaS tools, payment-provider fees, copywriting, product photography, ads and third-party usage often sit outside the build quote. A good agency tells you this before you ask.

## What a sensible Pakistan project range looks like

Our focused Shopify projects start from **PKR 40,000**. Focused custom software and MVP projects start from **PKR 150,000**. Those aren't comparable packages, and neither is a quote: the real range depends on screens and flows, integrations, roles, content, testing and post-launch support.

For a Pakistani business, ask specifically how the agency handles COD operations, local payment constraints, courier workflows, mobile performance, and whether they'll be awake during your working hours. A technically impressive build your team can't operate is a failed project with good screenshots.

## Why a written scope protects both sides

Most of our clients aren't technical, and they're used to agreeing a price up front for physical goods and services. A software quote should work the same way. You should know what you're paying before you commit, and we should know what we've promised.

What that means in practice:

- No surprise invoices.
- A written scope you can hold us to.
- If the project needs more work than we estimated, that's our mistake to absorb, not your bill to pay.

## Red flags worth taking seriously

- A very low quote with no written assumptions.
- A promise to build a large platform in an unrealistically short timeline.
- No straight answer about code, account or deployment ownership.
- A portfolio of screenshots with no explanation of the problem or the scope.
- Nobody named as responsible for communication after you've paid.
