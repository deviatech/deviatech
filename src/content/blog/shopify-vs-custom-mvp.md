We get asked this constantly: Shopify, WooCommerce, or build something custom?

The answer is almost always the least complicated option that supports how you sell today. For most Pakistani product businesses, that's Shopify or WooCommerce. Custom software is the right call less often than the people selling custom software suggest.

## The decision in one table

| | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom software |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Standard product checkout, fast launch | WordPress-based businesses wanting hosting control | A workflow that is itself the product |
| Time to launch | Days to weeks | Weeks | Weeks to months |
| Build starting point | From PKR 40,000 | Varies with hosting and plugin choices | From PKR 150,000 |
| Who maintains it | Shopify handles the platform | You (updates, backups, security, plugins) | You and your development partner |
| Recurring costs | Subscription, apps, transaction fees | Hosting, premium plugins, maintenance time | Hosting, third-party usage, ongoing development |
| Flexibility | Within Shopify's checkout and app rules | High, at the cost of maintenance | Whatever you can specify and pay for |
| COD in Pakistan | Workable, but an operations problem, not a checkbox | Workable, same operational requirements | Built exactly to your process |
| Avoid when | Your core value is not standard checkout | Nobody owns WordPress maintenance | A standard store already fits |

## Three questions that settle it

1. **Is your buying flow standard?** Browse, pick a variant, pay or confirm COD, wait for delivery. If that's your flow, a platform will do the job and custom software is premature.
2. **Who maintains this in six months?** If you can't name the person, choose the option that maintains itself. This question eliminates WooCommerce for a lot of businesses that would otherwise pick it.
3. **Is the workflow the reason customers choose you?** If yes, and only if yes, custom is worth the cost and the timeline.

## Where the table needs nuance

**Shopify's constraints are real but rarely fatal.** You work within its payment, app, theme and checkout rules. For clothing, food, gifts or beauty products, those rules are fine. Before committing, confirm the gateway, settlement, courier and tax workflow for your specific business, because that's the part that varies and the part nobody checks until launch week.

**WooCommerce's flexibility is a bill, not a feature.** It makes sense if you already run WordPress, want control over hosting and plugins, and have someone comfortable maintaining it. That last condition does the heavy lifting. Somebody owns updates, backups, security, plugin conflicts and performance from here on.

The usual mistake is comparing build quotes alone. WooCommerce often looks cheaper on day one and stops looking cheaper the first time a plugin conflict takes the checkout down on a Saturday.

**Custom software earns its price when the workflow is the value.** A marketplace, a complicated subscription rule, multi-vendor logistics: things that would be awkward workarounds on a platform. You also need someone who can make scope decisions and a budget for maintenance after launch, because custom software doesn't stop needing attention.

## The short version

If a standard buying flow gets you to your next real milestone, build the standard thing. If your workflow is why customers pick you, the cheaper-looking platform gets expensive through workarounds.
