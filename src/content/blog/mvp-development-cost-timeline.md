An MVP is not the cheapest version of a complete product. It is the smallest version that can produce useful evidence from real users. In Pakistan, a focused DeviaTech MVP project starts from **PKR 150,000**, and a typical delivery window is **3-10 weeks** depending on the core workflow, user roles, integrations, and review speed.

## What belongs in the first release

Start with the one thing the product needs to prove. That may be creating a resume, submitting a request, booking a service, managing a stock workflow, or coordinating a connected device. Authentication, basic admin, feedback collection, and error handling belong in version one when they are necessary to test that flow honestly.

Multiple dashboards, advanced permissions, every notification channel, complex billing, and visual polish that does not change the core behaviour can often wait.

## Where the budget goes

- Discovery, user flow, and scope definition.
- Product design for the key screens.
- Frontend and backend implementation.
- Authentication and essential admin tools.
- Integrations such as payments, email, SMS, AI, or external APIs.
- Testing, deployment, handoff, and the first support window.

Hosting, third-party usage, domain, content, and the founder's own time recruiting users are separate considerations.

## Why MVP timelines slip

The most common delays are not typing code. They are waiting for decisions, changing the core assumption, preparing data and content, adding another user role, and discovering that an integration does not work as expected. Agree on who decides, how weekly reviews happen, and what is explicitly outside the first scope.

## Pakistan-specific MVP decisions

Before building payments, phone verification, logistics, or messaging, ask whether that behaviour is part of the hypothesis. A marketplace may need local payment and delivery rules immediately; a content or workflow product may learn more by using a simpler contact or manual fulfilment step first.

## FAQ

### Can an MVP cost less than PKR 150,000?

Very small prototypes can, especially when the client supplies design, content, and technical direction. A working product with a reliable core flow, deployment, and handoff usually needs a more deliberate scope.

### Should I build a mobile app first?

Not by default. A responsive web product can validate behaviour more quickly. Choose native mobile first when device capabilities or usage patterns are central to the product.

### What happens after launch?

Collect evidence, fix blockers, and prioritise the next phase. Do not automatically build the original roadmap until users show that it deserves the investment.
